<p>Few things say Spain like a generous cheese board shared with friends. A good one is all about variety: one fresh cheese, one semi-cured and one well aged, plus something sweet, something salty and something to drink. Here is how we build ours.</p>

<h2>1. Choose three Spanish cheeses</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>A cured sheep's cheese:</strong> Manchego aged 6 to 12 months, nutty and firm. Our <a href="/products/los-cameros-blended-cheese-cured-6-months-world-cheese-award-2025-2026">Los Cameros cured 6 months</a> won at the World Cheese Awards.</li>
  <li><strong>A soft or creamy cheese:</strong> Tetilla from Galicia or a Torta del Casar to spread on bread.</li>
  <li><strong>Something with character:</strong> smoked Idiazábal from the Basque Country or a blue Cabrales for the brave.</li>
</ul>
<p>If you only want one cheese, make it a blend like <a href="/products/los-cameros-blended-cheese">Los Cameros Blended Cheese</a>: cow, sheep and goat milk in a single wedge.</p>

<h2>2. Add the sweet and salty extras</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Membrillo</strong> (quince paste): the classic partner for Manchego.</li>
  <li><strong>Marcona almonds</strong> and green olives.</li>
  <li><strong>Jamón or chorizo</strong>, thinly sliced.</li>
  <li>Grapes, figs or a drizzle of honey.</li>
  <li>Crackers, picos or a rustic loaf.</li>
</ul>

<h2>3. Put it together</h2>
<p>Take the cheeses out of the fridge 30 minutes before serving so they show all their flavour. Cut the Manchego into thin triangles, leave the soft cheese whole with its own knife and break the strong cheese into rough pieces. Place the cheeses first, then fill the gaps with the extras.</p>
<blockquote>Taste from mild to strong: start with the fresh cheese and finish with the most intense one.</blockquote>

<h2>4. Pair it with Spanish wine</h2>
<p>A young Rioja or Ribera del Duero works with cured sheep's cheese, a dry fino sherry is perfect with Manchego and almonds, and a sweet Pedro Ximénez is a treat with blue cheese.</p>
